# Scoping Lite Writing

## Apply Lite mode

Use Lite mode when the primary goal is efficient transfer of information. Typical cases include:

- technical and operational documentation;
- READMEs, architecture notes, specifications, policies, procedures, and reference material;
- reports, summaries, briefs, explanations, and troubleshooting text;
- professional prose where the user asks for concise, clear, direct, or lean writing;
- agent-facing instructions where shorter wording does not weaken trigger conditions or completion criteria.

## Bypass Lite mode

Do not use Lite mode when the requested style is itself part of the deliverable. This includes:

- fiction, narrative description, scene setting, dialogue, character voice, and diegetic prose;
- literary, poetic, comedic, dramatic, or highly stylized writing;
- brand or marketing copy governed by a defined voice;
- quotations, style exemplars, corpus samples, or wording that must be preserved;
- rewriting where the user explicitly asks to retain a voice that conflicts with Lite conventions.

## Mixed documents

Classify by passage, not only by file. Use Lite mode for explanatory scaffolding and ordinary documentation. Preserve embedded narrative, quotations, examples, or style-controlled passages under their own rules.

## Durable documentation

Lite mode does not mean chat shorthand. Durable documents still need enough context to stand alone. Keep complete sentences, explicit prerequisites, stable terminology, and the information a future reader needs without access to the current conversation.

If classification is uncertain, preserve the existing voice rather than flattening it.
