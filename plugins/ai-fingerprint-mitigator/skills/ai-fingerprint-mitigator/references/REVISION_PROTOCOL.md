# Revision Protocol

Use this order so style editing does not damage meaning.

1. Lock facts and constraints. Identify claims, citations, numbers, technical terms, names, exceptions, and required wording that cannot change.
2. Identify the target voice from the existing text, user samples, audience, and medium.
3. Mark repeated structural patterns: framing, paragraph templates, transitions, lists, triads, summaries, and repeated sentence openings.
4. Remove redundant scaffolding before changing vocabulary.
5. Replace vague language only where a more specific expression is supported by the source.
6. Rebuild sentence and paragraph rhythm around the actual ideas. Do not inject randomness.
7. Restore any nuance or uncertainty lost during compression.
8. Compare the revision with the source for semantic drift and voice drift.
9. Confirm the authorship boundaries remain intact.

Prefer one structural fix that removes several symptoms over many synonym substitutions. Word-swapping without structural revision often preserves the same formulaic shape.
